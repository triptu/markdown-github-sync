# On moving cities

*working title. maybe "The second room".*

## Opening

There's a particular kind of tired that comes from looking at apartment listings at midnight. It isn't sleepiness. It's the feeling of trying on twenty versions of your life in an hour and none of them fitting.

We've lived in this flat for four years. The kitchen is too small, the windows face a wall, and I love it in the way you love something you've stopped seeing. Meera says it's the best and worst place we've lived. She's right about both.

## Why move at all

The honest answer is that nothing is wrong. That's what makes it hard. When something is wrong, the decision makes itself. When nothing is wrong, you have to decide what *better* means, and then defend it against the voice that says you're being ungrateful.

Things I keep writing down:

- a second room, so one of us can close a door
- somewhere I can walk to water in under fifteen minutes
- a commute short enough that I don't need podcasts to survive it
- a neighborhood where the grocery store knows us

None of those are about the city. They're about the shape of a day.

## The rent math

I made a spreadsheet. Of course I made a spreadsheet.

| option | rent | rooms | walk to lake |
|---|---|---|---|
| stay | low | 1 + kitchen | 25 min |
| east side | medium | 2 | 10 min |
| new city | medium-high | 2 | ? |

The east side works if we cook more and stop pretending we'll use the gym. The new city works only if the job does, and the job is the one thing I can't put in a cell.

I wrote once that [[2026030820 Rent is a vote for a life]]. I still think so. The trouble is I want to vote for two lives at once.

## What the old flat taught me

- you can be happy in a small space if the light is good
- you cannot be happy in a small space if you both work from it
- a window that faces a wall makes you go outside more, which is maybe a gift

## The fear part

*(this section is the real essay, i think. everything above is warm-up.)*

I'm scared of moving because I'm scared the new place will be fine. Not bad. Fine. And then I'll have spent the energy and the money and the boxes and the goodbyes on *fine*.

But staying is also a choice, and it's also going to be fine.

So the question isn't which is better. It's which version of fine I'd rather wake up in.
